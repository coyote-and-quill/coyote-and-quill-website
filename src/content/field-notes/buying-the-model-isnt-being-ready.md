Part of what Coyote & Quill is for: we watch the frontier so you don't have to, then tell you what actually matters.

Here's one worth 28 minutes. Greg Brockman — OpenAI's president — sat down with a room of founders. [Watch it here.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bBS93A0BeNI)

**Why it's worth your time:** the man whose company sells the model spends most of the hour telling founders the model isn't the hard part. That candor, from that seat, is rare — and it points straight at where AI projects actually succeed or fail.

Here's what we took from it, and why it's exactly how we think about readiness at C&Q.

## The model is one-eighth of the job

Brockman keeps circling one idea without naming it: **readiness is not one thing you buy. It's a stack of things you have to get right at once.**

The military has a checklist for exactly this. Before it fields a new capability, it doesn't just ask "did we buy the kit?" It runs down eight dimensions — doctrine, organization, training, materiel, leadership, personnel, facilities, policy. The kit — *materiel* — is one of the eight. Buy the shiny new system and neglect the other seven, and you are not ready. You just spent money.

The acronym is DOTMLPF-P. Ugly; the idea is clean — **the equipment is the easy part; readiness lives in everything around it.** Listen to Brockman again with the eight in mind:

- **Doctrine** — he requires "a human to be accountable for all code that gets merged." Do you have a *written* rule for who signs off on AI work, or is it vibes?
- **Organization** — OpenAI is "still figuring it out" on how to structure teams around agents. Who owns AI enablement at your shop?
- **Training** — "it's very different to hear AI described versus to use it." Are your people trained to *operate* it, not just fear it?
- **Materiel** — the model, the tools. The one box most people think is the whole test. It's one-eighth.
- **Leadership** — "human attention is going to be this incredibly scarce resource." Do your leaders know where to spend theirs?
- **Personnel** — the room asks whether we'll have "human software engineers in a decade." Which of your roles shift from *doing* to *deciding*?
- **Facilities** — data provenance, permissions, the plumbing. When the AI touches a document, can you trace what it did?
- **Policy** — "in cybersecurity and in biosecurity, those are areas where we're very thoughtful." In regulated work, the human sign-off isn't a courtesy. It's the law.

Brockman, whose job is to sell you dimension four, spent the hour on the other seven.

## Why this is how we work

This is the exact framework Coyote & Quill runs an AI-readiness assessment on: we check all eight *before* you pour money into the one. The failure mode isn't buying the wrong model — it's buying the right model into an organization that was never ready to hold it.

So when the president of the leading AI lab spends an hour making that case to a room of founders, we take it as confirmation, not news. It's the thing we assess for — and staying close to what the frontier is actually doing is how we keep that assessment honest.

**You don't get ready by shopping. You get ready by checking the other seven boxes.**
